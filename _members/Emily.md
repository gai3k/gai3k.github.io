Emily Huie is a Neuroscience PhD student at the Mayo Clinic Graduate School of Biomedical Sciences. She earned her Bachelor of Science in Neurobiology with a minor in Public Health from the University of California, Davis.
During her undergraduate studies, she joined the Dugger Laboratory at the UC Davis School of Medicine, where she investigated the neuropathology of Alzheimer’s disease with a focus on TDP-43.
After graduation, she worked as a research technician in the Silverman Laboratory at the UC Davis MIND Institute, conducting behavioral pharmacology experiments to identify potential treatments for rare neurodevelopmental disorders.
Emily is interested in understanding the molecular mechanisms driving the interactions between the central nervous system and glioblastoma, with the goal of identifying new therapeutic targets that could improve patient prognosis.
Outside of the lab, Emily enjoys making music with friends, playing tennis, improvising on the piano, painting, and spending time in nature. She also loves traveling to new places and hopes to find the best ramen in Phoenix, Arizona.
